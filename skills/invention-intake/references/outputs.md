# Output conventions

Deliver only what is requested; combine related artifacts when useful. Markdown is the default. Use stable headings and source IDs so counsel can revise the result.

## Disclosure

Include a working title, review status, source register, plain-language explanation, technical problem, inventor-described existing approach, proposed mechanism, component roles, process steps, asserted contribution, supported/claimed advantages, alternatives, and unresolved issues. Record reported contributors without a legal inventorship conclusion.

Cite technical assertions at the paragraph or table-row level. Distinguish source-supported statements, inventor confirmation, inferences, proposed alternatives, and unknowns. Add a material-change note on revisions.

## Follow-up questions

Prioritize critical technical gaps and contradictions. Tie each question to a source or identified unknown and explain the practical consequence of its answer. Do not ask for optional corporate information before clarifying the invention's mechanism unless the user's requested analysis depends on it.

## Diagrams

Mermaid or a textual diagram is sufficient. Put supporting sources in the caption. Use exact source terminology where possible. Show uncertainty in the diagram labels or caption, not solely in a distant footnote. Do not draw an unsupported execution path as established behavior. If order or architecture is unresolved, show the supported partial model and explain what cannot yet be drawn.

## Filing-review packet

Summarize technical readiness, strategic fit, supplied portfolio candidates, business criteria, legal questions for counsel, cost assumptions, missing inputs, and next actions. Identify who must resolve each issue when known. Clearly distinguish the agent's analysis from inventor statements and counsel decisions.

If the user supplies a scoring rubric, preserve its definitions, weights, and scale. Do not silently create thresholds. If a rubric is proposed, label it as a draft policy for approval and do not present it as the company's policy.

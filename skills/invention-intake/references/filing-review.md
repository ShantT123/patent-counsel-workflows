# Filing review

## Strategy and business criteria

Map the invention to supplied technology buckets, roadmap initiatives, and responsible teams. Explain the mapping and evidence. If strategy is absent, offer a provisional technical category clearly labeled as an inference; do not claim it fills a known company gap.

Apply only supplied business criteria. Common dimensions include product relevance, likely competitor use, detectability, and strategic differentiation. Separate a score from the evidence and uncertainty supporting it. Unknown values remain unknown; do not count them as zero or compute a complete ranking from missing data without an explicitly agreed method.

Potential claim breadth, eligibility, novelty, nonobviousness, support, and inventorship are questions for substantiated counsel assessment. Do not predict allowance from a disclosure's polish or a business score.

## Portfolio

For supplied records, preserve publication/application/patent identifiers, family relationships, jurisdictions, dates, and status as actually known. Do not equate publication with issuance or assume a pending claim has issued scope. Do not count family members as separate inventions without explaining the counting basis.

A shared title, abstract, or product label supports a candidate-overlap finding only. Explain what claims and status records counsel would need to assess coverage. A missing supplied record does not establish a portfolio gap. Portfolio fit is not freedom to operate.

## Costs

Use supplied currency, time period, unit costs, complexity assumptions, and inclusions. Distinguish preparation, official fees, prosecution, foreign filings, and maintenance when the data supports it. Show quantities and arithmetic; use deterministic calculation tools when available. A count multiplied by a unit cost is an estimate, not a legal budget guarantee.

Do not suggest deferral or consolidation as legally safe. Flag timing and subject-matter consequences for counsel before a budget-driven decision. Without costs or budget data, list what is needed and leave the estimate unavailable.

## Primary references for maintainers

These are starting points for source checking, not an embedded current-law digest. Check current primary authority before giving jurisdiction-specific legal analysis; state jurisdiction and date. This intake skill does not make that analysis automatically.

- USPTO subject matter eligibility: https://www.uspto.gov/patents/laws/examination-policy/subject-matter-eligibility
- USPTO AI tools practice guidance announcement: https://www.uspto.gov/about-us/news-updates/uspto-issues-guidance-concerning-use-ai-tools-parties-and-practitioners

USPTO guidance is not a substitute for governing statutes and case law. A maintainer adding legal content should identify the underlying authority, its date, and its limits.

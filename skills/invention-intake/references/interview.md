# Technical interview guidance

Adapt questions to the materials. Do not deliver this whole list as a questionnaire.

## Mechanism and contribution

- What input enters the system, which components transform it, and what output results?
- What precisely changes compared with the approach the team used before?
- Which steps, relationships, and timing constraints make the technique work?
- What does the inventor mean by "smart," "adaptive," "optimized," or similar result-oriented language?
- What new capability becomes possible in the larger system because of this mechanism?

## Implementation and support

- Walk through one concrete execution with example values or state transitions.
- Which details are implemented, tested, planned, or hypothetical?
- What happens on failure, stale data, concurrency, boundary conditions, or incompatible versions?
- Which components are essential, and which could be replaced? Why?
- Are diagrams consistent with the narrative and actual data/control flow?

## Advantages and alternatives

- What baseline supports the comparison? What was measured, under which conditions?
- What alternatives did the team try, and what tradeoffs did they observe?
- Could a materially different implementation achieve the same result?
- Which claimed advantages are expectations rather than observations?

## Counsel intake

Ask about reported contributors and their specific contributions without deciding inventorship. Ask about known related work, disclosures, demonstrations, release plans, offers, and sales when relevant. Preserve exact dates and uncertainties for counsel. Do not assure the user that a planned delay is legally safe.

For each prioritized follow-up, include: the question, why it matters, the source gap/conflict it addresses, and who could answer it if known.

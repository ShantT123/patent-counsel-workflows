# Evidence and uncertainty

## Source register

Assign each source a stable ID, title, date when known, and accessible locator. Use page/paragraph, section, transcript timestamp, or numbered note. For unnumbered notes, create and retain paragraph identifiers. Preserve supplied IDs. State whether a source was readable in full, partially readable, or inaccessible.

Cite assertions as `[SRC-01 §2]` or another unambiguous locator. A citation must actually support the adjacent assertion. Do not cite an entire folder as support for a specific mechanism. If no locator is available, identify the source and explain the limitation.

## Status vocabulary

- **Source-supported:** accurately reflects supplied material; not independent verification of its truth.
- **Inventor-confirmed:** explicitly confirmed in a recorded or supplied inventor response, with a locator; still not independently verified.
- **Inference:** analyst interpretation, with reasoning and supporting sources.
- **Proposed alternative:** a possibility to discuss; not attributed to an inventor or treated as implemented.
- **Unknown:** not established by the supplied evidence.

Use status labels wherever the distinction changes a reader's understanding. Quantified results need conditions, units, baseline, and evidence. A claimed advantage without measurements remains an inventor assertion. Do not invent results, source passages, components, or implementation details.

## Conflicts and changes

Preserve competing statements with their locators. Do not silently prefer a later document unless the evidence establishes it supersedes the earlier version. Mark what answer would resolve the conflict. Record subsequent confirmation as a new source rather than rewriting history.

## Human and AI contributions

Preserve the inventor's reported mechanism. Put AI-generated extensions in a separate proposed-alternatives section and request inventor/counsel review. Do not characterize an AI suggestion as the inventor's original contribution. Report contribution accounts without reaching a legal inventorship conclusion.

## External material

If external research is requested and available, distinguish retrieved material from internal sources; include publication/access dates when available and actual document locators. Never fabricate a patent publication number, claim quotation, or search result. A limited search is not an exhaustive search or legal clearance.
